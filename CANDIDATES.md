# Tagline candidates — One Line, One Week: The make.fun Tagline

Scored against the judge brief's 5 criteria (each out of 5, total out of 25),
most important first:

1. Reads as a single-line tagline for make.fun.
2. True and specific about what make.fun does: agents enter bounties and the
   best entries win the reward.
3. Lands in one read, no clause to untangle.
4. Worth repeating — a turn of phrase or rhythm that sticks.
5. Inside the character limit, no hype or empty superlatives.

Notes on the scoring:

- **#3 (24/25, strongest)** — the build/enter/win triplet is the most repeatable
  rhythm in the set, and it lands in one read. Its only weakness is C2: "compete
  for real bounties" says agents chase bounties but never states that the *best
  entry* wins the reward, and "real" is filler.
- **#1, #4, #5, #7 (23)** — all specific and clean, but plainer: #1 and #4 read
  like statements rather than lines worth repeating (C4 3); #5 drops the agent;
  #7's "one winner" clause needs a beat to untangle (C3 4).
- **#2, #6 (22)** — specific, but the em-dash clause (#2) and the three clipped
  sentences (#6) cost a point on landing in one read, and neither has a
  memorable turn of phrase.
- **#8 (22)** — the snappiest rhythm, but it could sell any contest (C1 4, C2 3):
  no agents, no make.fun, no "best entry wins".

## Pick and revision

**Picked: #3.** Then revised at least once before submitting (the revision below
is the version in `main.py`):

- Before: `Build it, enter it, win it: AI agents compete for real bounties on make.fun.` (76 chars)
- After: `Build it, enter it, win it: on make.fun, agents enter bounties and the best entry takes the reward.` (99 chars)

What the revision changed: the second half was rewritten from "AI agents compete
for real bounties on make.fun" to "on make.fun, agents enter bounties and the
best entry takes the reward". It keeps the triplet rhythm and the brand name,
drops the vague filler "real", and now states criterion 2 in full — agents
*enter* bounties, and the *best entry takes the reward* — using the brief's own
words. Final line: 99 characters, single line, no hype, no superlatives.
